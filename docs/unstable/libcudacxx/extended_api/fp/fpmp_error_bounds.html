Error Bounds for the Basic Operations#

This page states what the fpmp2 arithmetic guarantees, operation by operation. Each bound is given in units of u², where u is the unit roundoff of the limb format, because that is the natural scale of a double-word result: the limbs hold 2p bits, and the best any pair operation can do is a few units in the last of them.

Conventions#

  • The error of an operation is |computed - exact| / |exact| (relative) or |computed - exact| (absolute), where computed is the exact real value hi + lo of the result pair and exact is the mathematically exact result for the exact real values of the operands. Nothing is rounded to a single float or double on the way.

  • Operands are normalized pairs, |lo| <= ulp(hi)/2 — the state every mid and high operation leaves its result in. The low level does not normalize; see below.

  • No overflow and no underflow occurs in either limb, in the operands, the result or the intermediates, and no operand is a NaN or an infinity. Behaviour on special values is in the specification.

  • The bounds are first order: terms in u³ and beyond are dropped where the source does so, and shown where the source keeps them.

  • Host and device run the same operation sequences, and differ only in the seed that starts the div and sqrt iterations: for fp32mp2 the device uses the approximate reciprocal and reciprocal-square-root instructions and the host an exact division; for fp64mp2 both reciprocals are correctly rounded, while the device’s reciprocal square root is close to correctly rounded and the host’s 1/sqrt(x) carries two roundings. One Newton step leaves about (3/2)·ε² of a seed error ε, which at double-word precision is visible: a 1u seed leaves 1.5u², a 1.5u seed 3.4u², a 2u seed 6.5u². Where the sides differ, the tables quote the device.

The bounds#

Status says where a bound comes from: proven — a published theorem about the same operation sequence, formally verified in Coq by Muller and Rideau; derived — first-order error accounting over the sequence as implemented, not formally verified; measured — no proof, the figure is the largest error seen. Measured columns give the largest relative error observed, in units of u², over 224 operands from each operation’s working range on the device (the runs that feed the specification) and 224 per regime on the host against binary128 — whichever of the two is larger.

low and mid addition, and low and mid fma, compute the same value; mid additionally normalizes it, which is exact, so they share a bound and differ only in the state of the result. The division and square-root sequences (Nagai et al. 2008 and Karp-Markstein 1997 iterations) have no published analysis; double-word divisions with proven bounds exist ([JMP17] Algorithm 18, 9.8u² at 31 operations) but are not the ones implemented.

Why some bounds are absolute: cancellation#

A double-word addition computes the sum of the high limbs exactly, then adds the low limbs with one or two roundings. Those roundings are small compared with the operands — a few u² of |x| + |y| — but when x and y nearly cancel, the result is far smaller than the operands, and an error that is tiny against |x| + |y| can be the whole result. The default addition commits its roundings before it knows whether the high limbs cancelled, so its relative error has no bound at all — [JMP17] exhibits operands for which it equals 1, a result with no correct digit, and its recommendation is to use this algorithm only when both operands are known to have the same sign. The high level orders the work so that the error is committed against the result, which is what its unconditional 3u² buys.

The same happens to fma at every level. Its residual, the exact error of the hardware fma(x_hi, y_hi, z_hi), is recovered from pieces whose roundings are relative to |x·y| and |z|, not to x·y + z. When x·y ≈ -z there is no level that restores a relative guarantee; the absolute bound is the statement, and it is still a few u² of the larger operand.

Concretely, with fp32mp2: take x = (1, -2⁻²⁴) and y = (-1 + 2⁻²³, -2⁻²⁶ + 2⁻⁴⁹), both normalized pairs. The high limbs cancel to 2⁻²³, the exact sum is 1.5·2⁻²⁵ + 2⁻⁴⁹, and the default addition returns 1.5·2⁻²⁵ exactly: the 2⁻⁴⁹ was rounded away when the low limbs were added, before the cancellation was known. That is a relative error of 4.0e-8, about 25 correct bits from a format that carries 46, and well inside the absolute bound (2⁻⁴⁹ against 3u²·(|x| + |y|) ≈ 2⁻⁴⁵). add<fpmp2_accuracy::high>(x, y) returns (1.5·2⁻²⁵, 2⁻⁴⁹), the exact sum. If a difference in your code can cancel and its relative accuracy matters, compute it at high; the cost is a few extra FP32 operations on that one step, as Accuracy levels shows.

The low level, and composing operations#

Every bound assumes normalized operands. A low result is not normalized — its limbs may overlap — and feeding it to a further operation weakens that operation’s bound by roughly the overlap: an input with |lo| ≤ k·u·|hi| instead of u·|hi| scales the u² constants of the next operation by about k. Over a long low chain the overlap grows with the chain, which is why renormalize() exists and why converting a low value to mid or high restores the invariant (changing the accuracy level). The fpmp2_stat types measure the overlap on real data.

Errors of successive operations add to first order: a sequence of n operations with constants c₁ … cₙ has relative error at most (c₁ + … + cₙ)·u² as long as no step cancels and every input is normalized. A step that cancels contributes its absolute bound to the running error, which is what makes the relative error of a long computation depend on the data rather than on the operation count.

How the figures were obtained#

The measured columns come from the same harness that produces the per-function tables of the specification: 224 random operands per test over the operation’s working range (and 232 structured bit patterns, whose maxima are dominated by subnormal operands and are not quoted here), compared with a quad-precision reference computed on the device. For fp64mp2 the reference is binary128, which carries seven more bits than the pair; its own rounding puts an uncertainty of about one percent on the fp64mp2 figures. Nothing on this page comes from a formal verification of the CCCL sources themselves: proven means the operation sequence is the one the cited theorem is about. Where the literature gives the worst constructible case — 2u² for the accumulate, 3u² for the high addition, 4.986u² for the multiply at p = 24 — the device maxima above sit within a few percent of it, which is as close as random sampling gets to inputs that have to be built by hand.
